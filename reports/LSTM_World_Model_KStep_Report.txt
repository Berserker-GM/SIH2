1. PURPOSE
----------
The previous training step learned one-step dynamics:

    P(S_(t+1) | S_t, ..., S_(t-L+1))

and a binary head P(attack in the next K=6 windows) (~30 seconds).

That binary head is NOT a K-step forecast. It does not simulate future
states. This step evaluates the actual world-model claim:

    Closed-loop rollout:  S_t → Ŝ_(t+1) → Ŝ_(t+2) → ... → Ŝ_(t+K)

K = 6 windows at Δt = 5 s  ⇒  30-second trajectory.

The LSTM weights were NOT updated. Architecture was NOT changed.
The saved checkpoint from multi-day training was reused.


2. WHAT WAS EVALUATED
---------------------
Closed-loop (teacher-free):
  history x = [S_(t-7), ..., S_t]          length L = 8, dim = 32
  Ŝ_(t+1) = next_state_head(LSTM(x))
  x ← [S_(t-6), ..., S_t, Ŝ_(t+1)]
  repeat until Ŝ_(t+K)

Persist-rollout baseline (required SIH comparison):
  Ŝ_(t+k) = S_t  for all k = 1..6

A K-step window is kept only if:
  - all history + future pairs share the same day_id
  - timestamps are strictly increasing
  - adjacent deltas ≤ 15 s (same rule as 1-step pair generation)
  Sequences must not cross a day boundary or a large gap.

6. INTERPRETATION
-----------------
What the K-step step proved
  - The saved next-state head can be unrolled for 30 seconds without
    becoming unstable on web and infiltration traffic.
  - On 23 Feb val and 01 Mar test, closed-loop MSE is ~40% below persist
    at every k, including k=6.
  - Day-boundary / gap filters still hold for multi-step futures
    (0 order errors; 24 day-boundary rejects on test).

What it did not prove
  - It is not a volumetric-DDoS trajectory model. 21 Feb HOIC is outside
    the train scaler; rollout cannot invent z=489 from z≈0 predictions.
  - The attack head was not used here. Binary attack-within-K is still
    a separate 1-shot classifier, not a simulated MITRE chain.

Honest SIH wording:
  The world model forecasts network state 30 seconds ahead better than
  persistence on held-out web and infiltration days. K-step MSE on
  unseen HOIC is dominated by count-feature scale shift, not by
  exploding rollouts.
