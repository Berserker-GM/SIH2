-------------------------
This report explains EVERY panel on the live CSV dashboard (the screen that
replaced the old SOC “NORMATIVE SOC OPERATOR” skin).

It also maps each panel to the SIH problem statement:

  CSV / PCAP / live telemetry
          ↓
     extract state S_t          (32-d feature VECTOR every 5s)
          ↓
       LSTM                     (sequence model of P(S_{t+1} | history))
          ↓
  predict next 30 seconds       (closed-loop K=6)
          ↓
  attack probability + MITRE stage + WHY
          ↓
      dashboard

What you are looking at in the screenshots is NOT the old hardcoded 01 Mar
replay JSON. It is:

  CIC-shaped CSV  →  5s windows  →  last 8 × 32  →  frozen LSTM  →  live JSON

Frozen on purpose (must not change):
  world_lstm.pt, scaler.npz, LSTM 2×64, input_dim=32, seq_len=8,
  K=6, attack threshold 0.15. No retraining for this report.

3. TOP ROW — THE THREE SIH ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The problem asks the system to “forecast future network states and
estimate the probability of attacker progression” and “map predicted
behaviour to recognised attack stages (e.g. MITRE ATT&CK).”

These three cards are that sentence.

3.1 ATTACK IN 30s
    Big number: 0.0%
    Bar: P / 0.15, cyan when below threshold, red when alert.
    Footer: “P=0.000 · threshold 0.15 · below threshold”

    What it is mathematically:
      The LSTM has two heads.
      Head B emits a logit for “any attack in the next K=6 windows.”
      P = sigmoid(logit) on the CURRENT 8-step history (not on Ŝ).
      something_bad = (P ≥ 0.15)

    What it is NOT:
      Not a per-flow IDS label.
      Not the old SOC “CRITICAL THREATS: 7” mock counter.

3.2 MITRE STAGE
    Screenshot: “Benign” (no technique id, because benign has none)
    Subtext: synthetic_demo.csv · 23 windows · last 8 fed to LSTM

    What it is:
      After closed-loop rollout, Ŝ_{t+6} (the state 30s ahead) is
      decoded by a multinomial Logistic Regression trained ONLY on
      train-day scaled windows:
        benign, reconnaissance, initial_access, lateral_movement,
        command_and_control, exfiltration (reserved), impact (DoS)

      Representative techniques:
        reconnaissance     T1046  Network Service Discovery
        initial_access     T1110  Brute Force
        lateral_movement   T1021  Remote Services
        command_and_control T1071 Application Layer Protocol
        exfiltration       T1048  (reserved — CIC has no exfil label)
        impact             T1498  Network Denial of Service

    Decoder honesty: command_and_control was held out of train (02 Mar
    bot). The decoder cannot invent C2 if it never saw it. Quote that.

3.3 NOW (S_t)
    Timestamp of the LAST observed 5s bin, here
      2018-02-28T10:01:50+00:00
    Footer: source=sample · seq 8×32 · horizon 30s

    S_t is the 32-d feature VECTOR at time t. That is Section 2 of the
    problem: “structured state vector at time t.” The 8×32 block ending
    at S_t is the LSTM input x = [S_{t-7}, …, S_t].

6. 30s TIMELINE (CLOSED-LOOP Ŝ)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Screenshot: six rows, all “benign”, P=0.000

    t+5s    benign    P=0.000
    t+10s   benign    P=0.000
    t+15s   benign    P=0.000
    t+20s   benign    P=0.000
    t+25s   benign    P=0.000
    t+30s   benign    P=0.000

How it is built (the actual world-model use):

  Ŝ_{t+1} = HeadA(LSTM([S_{t-7}, …, S_t]))
  x ← [S_{t-6}, …, S_t, Ŝ_{t+1}]
  repeat until k=6

Each Ŝ_{t+k} is:
  - decoded to a MITRE stage (LogReg)
  - paired with the per-step attack probability from Head B during rollout
  - stamped t_now + 5k seconds

This is “forward simulation K steps” and “probability of attacker
progression” from the problem statement. Persistence baseline
(Ŝ = copy S_t) is what we beat on 01 Mar; the dashboard shows the
LSTM rollout, not persist.

7. THREE “WHY” COLUMNS (FEATURE ATTRIBUTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each list is top-5 features with a signed score and ▲ / ▼.

7.1 Why · attack head
    Screenshot (synthetic sample):
      down_up_ratio_mean   ▲ 0.047
      bytes_fwd_sum        ▲ 0.032
      init_bwd_win_mean    ▲ 0.031
      (plus two more)

    Formula: mean |∂ attack_logit / ∂ x| over the 8 history steps.
    High score = that coordinate of the 32-d vector most moves the
    “something bad in 30s” logit. Direction comes from the signed
    gradient (▲ up, ▼ down).

7.2 Why · MITRE stage
    Screenshot:
      psh_flag_sum         ▲ 1.368
      pkt_len_max          ▲ 1.170
      dest_port_entropy    ▲ 0.969

    Formula: LogReg coef[stage] × Ŝ_{t+6}   (linear contribution).
    Answers: “which features of the *predicted* state made the decoder
    say this MITRE stage?”

7.3 Why · 30s change
    Screenshot:
      pkt_len_mean         ▼ 6.273
      duration_max         ▼ 1.623
      tcp_ratio            ▲ 1.354

    Formula: Ŝ_{t+6} − S_t   (what the forecast thinks will MOVE).
    This is dynamics explainability, not classification explainability.
    On the sample, the model expects packet length / duration to drop
    and tcp_ratio to rise a little — small benign drift.

These three columns together are the SIH explainability requirement
without adding a SHAP package or LSTM attention weights (those remain
unchecked; must not change the frozen architecture to insert attention).

9. HISTORY TABLE  (8 × 32 FED TO THE LSTM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Title: “History fed to LSTM (8 × 32, unscaled)”

Rows: t-7, t-6, …, t-1, t
  t is highlighted (cyan). It MUST match the 32-d grid above.

Columns: the same 32 feature names, in STATE_FEATURE_ORDER.

This is the tensor x ∈ R^{8×32} after windowing, BEFORE the train
scaler. The LSTM sees scaler.transform(x).

On the sample, flow_count is 3.00 on most rows and 5.00 on t-6 / t-5 —
the synthetic generator briefly emitted more flows, then settled.
That is temporal structure, which a single-row classifier would miss
and the LSTM is trained to use.

seq_len=8 was chosen so 40s of past (8×5s) predicts 30s of future
(6×5s). Do not change seq_len without retraining (must).
