1. PURPOSE
----------
Map one history window to three sentences without retraining:

  Something bad is likely to happen?   P(attack within 30s) vs 0.15
  What is likely to happen?            MITRE stage of Ŝ_(t+6)
  Why does the model think that?       feature-level reasons

LSTM architecture and weights were not changed. Thresholds 0.15 / 0.05
were not changed.


2. METHOD (no extra Python packages)
------------------------------------
why_attack
  Input saliency of the existing attack head:
  mean over the 8-step history of |∂ logit / ∂ x_j|.
  Answers: which current 32-d features push "something bad".

why_stage
  Linear LogReg contribution on the forecast state:
  coef[stage] * Ŝ_(t+6).
  Answers: which forecast features make the decoder emit that MITRE stage.

why_change
  Ŝ_(t+6) − S_t  (scaled space, same as the LSTM).
  Answers: what the world model thinks will move in 30 seconds.

Narrative is assembled from the top feature of each list.

4. 01 MAR EXAMPLES (first 8 non-benign-stage windows)
-----------------------------------------------------
Scanned 347 k-step windows; dumped 8. All eight:

  something_bad = False   (attack P from 0.00 to 0.13, all < 0.15)
  stage           = lateral_movement (T1021 Remote Services)

Typical narrative:
  "No attack alert (probability 0.09 < 0.15). What is likely:
   lateral_movement (T1021 Remote Services). Why: the attack head is
   driven most by pkt_len_max; the stage decode leans on port_scan_score;
   the 30s forecast changes dest_port_entropy most."

This disagreement is real, not a bug:
  - The binary head was calibrated on val F1 (threshold 0.15) and is
    conservative on these 01 Mar prefixes.
  - The stage decoder, looking at Ŝ_(t+6), already reads infiltration
    (the 01 Mar MITRE win from the previous report).
  SIH wording: the world model can say WHAT before the binary alarm
  crosses the official threshold. Show both, do not hide the gap.
